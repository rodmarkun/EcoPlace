# Independent synthetic scenario design — 9 September 2026

**Constructed and structurally checked; not calibrated or launched.** This is question B
from [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md). Exact inputs are generated by `independent_scenarios.py` into
`independent_scenarios_20260909_v1/`. There are twelve base configurations and a `DESIGN.json`
containing their hashes, thirty-six effective bundle/config identities, network construction,
reservation witnesses, fixed-reference sources and calibration/pilot seeds. No policy has been
evaluated on this family. It is not a preregistration or a replacement for unfinished P1/P2.

The construction is new and does not reuse Alibaba fit records, sampled call trees or the old
tight/drift/wide topology. Its author has already inspected historical policy results and knows
the strong simple reference; it is not independent of that knowledge. The inputs below are
explicit synthetic choices, not measurements of another production deployment. Their purpose
is to test whether practical value survives a changed, inspectable operating setting, including
an already strong simple policy. A flat or negative result is an acceptable research result.

## Operational questions and fixed construction

The family crosses two placements of infrastructure capacity with two traffic profiles and
three offered-demand levels. It asks whether a rule transfers between a more distributed fog
infrastructure and an edge-heavy infrastructure, and between constant and time-concentrated
traffic of equal total volume. Every condition has 18 schedulable nodes. These are not
proportional replicas of the historical 15-node fixture.

| Topology | Cloud nodes | Sites | Fog/site | Edge/site | Total cloud/fog/edge |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| distributed | 3 | 3 | 2 | 3 | 3 / 6 / 9 |
| edge_heavy | 2 | 4 | 1 | 3 | 2 / 4 / 12 |

Cloud nodes each reserve 16000 model millicores and 32768 MiB; fog memory is 8192 MiB;
edge memory is 4096 MiB. All nodes start active. Far-edge CPU capacity is 4000 per node.
The three existing development bundles bind fog power and capacity jointly: 357193,
77191 and 4000 model millicores per node. This retains the declared cross-domain capacity
assumption and does not independently validate it. The family does not claim a power-only
intervention. Published power/transition and allocation source records are carried in the
bundle manifests, with the same limitations as the historical parameter appendix.

Each cloud node connects to a WAN vertex by 5 ms; site hub `s` connects to it by `15 + 5s`
ms, with sites numbered from zero. Fog and edge nodes attach to their own hub by 1 and 2 ms,
respectively. Links are undirected. The complete one-way latency matrix is the shortest-path
metric of this tree network; hubs are not schedulable nodes. This gives 2 ms fog/fog,
3 ms fog/edge, and 4 ms edge/edge latency within a site. Full node IDs, links and matrices
are retained. Geography is represented through inter-node latency only: there is no modeled
client-origin affinity or new bandwidth-congestion model.

Six services use directly specified lognormal processing times. `mu = log(mean) - log(1+CV²)/2`
and `sigma = sqrt(log(1+CV²))`. Means below are seconds; CPU demand is held while processing
each invocation, separately from the replica's scheduling request. These are model inputs,
not empirical response-time fits. No former 0.45 attribution transform is applied.

| ID | Service | Mean s | CV | CPU request | Busy CPU demand | Memory MiB |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | gateway | 0.006 | 0.25 | 50 | 500 | 128 |
| 1 | lookup | 0.018 | 0.50 | 500 | 1000 | 256 |
| 2 | profile | 0.030 | 0.75 | 500 | 1500 | 512 |
| 3 | inventory | 0.012 | 0.25 | 250 | 500 | 256 |
| 4 | compute | 0.060 | 0.75 | 1000 | 2000 | 1024 |
| 5 | commit | 0.024 | 0.50 | 500 | 1000 | 512 |

The gateway has four initial/minimum replicas and sixteen maximum replicas. Other services
have two initial/minimum and eight maximum replicas. Each has two server slots per
replica and a queue capacity of 32. Cold start is 0.2 s; image size is 20 MiB. No service is
stateful, PDB-protected or attached to an external backend. Every call is blocking, with edge
probability one, no edge timeout/retry, and request/response sizes 2/4 in the schema's KB units.
Blocking callers hold a server slot while waiting for their children. Repeated inventory calls
are separate invocations, not a graph-deduplication artifact.

Before any calibration, a controller-level review corrected the first draft's gateway request
of 250 and replica range 2–8. CPU-based HPA sees the gateway's own processing work, not its
blocked server occupancy. Under unconstrained throughput `r`, its expected CPU-driven replica
target was only `r × 0.006 × 500 / (0.6 × 250) = 0.02r`, allowing blocking saturation without
a sufficient scaling signal. The revised request gives `0.1r`, bounded by 4–16 replicas.
This is a structural design calculation, not a QoS guarantee or a claim about stochastic
HPA trajectories. The original code, document, tests and generated inputs are preserved under
`independent_scenarios_setup_20260909/gateway-reservation-first-draft/`; no policy outcomes
were generated or consulted for the correction. Calibration/pilot seed roles are unchanged.

| Template | Request mix | Full call tree | Deadline s | Own processing CPU-work/request |
|---|---:|---|---:|---:|
| interactive | 0.5 | `0(1,2(3))` | 0.3 | 72 |
| transaction | 0.3 | `0(4(5))` | 0.5 | 147 |

CPU work is measured in model-millicore seconds. The mix averages 119.7 per offered request.
The deadlines are declared synthetic application budgets, unchanged across all twelve cells;
they were not adjusted against policy output. They are not sums of stage percentiles or
observed production requirements. All three attainment targets are 0.90, goodput target 0.95,
and drop and timeout caps are each 0.025. Migration caps are 1024 MiB, 32 moves and 120 s
unavailability. Energy weights are each one; boundary ceilings use at most 64 transitions per
node and 20 GiB network bytes. These caps do not state measured real-world operating limits.

## Demand grid and controllers

Episodes last 900 s. Arrivals are independent template Poisson processes beginning at 30 s
and cut off at 870 s. The final 30 s is drain time; unfinished requests remain represented
under the existing evaluator accounting. The pressure parameter is **offered own-processing
CPU work divided by total far-edge capacity and the 840 s arrival window**. It is not measured
utilization, a stability proof or the load on a specific placement. It ignores blocking-server
occupancy, waiting, startup and dropped/unfinished invocations.

For pressure `q` in `{0.15, 0.30, 0.45}`, steady aggregate request rate is
`q × total_far_edge_capacity / 119.7`. The three template rates follow the fixed mix above.
Thus the distributed steady aggregate rates are approximately 45.113, 90.226 and 135.338 /s;
edge-heavy rates are 60.150, 120.301 and 180.451 /s. JSON contains exact floating-point values.

| Interval s | Steady multiplier | Two-burst multiplier |
|---|---:|---:|
| 30–270 | 1 | 0.75 |
| 270–330 | 1 | 2.5 |
| 330–570 | 1 | 0.75 |
| 570–690 | 1 | 1.625 |
| 690–870 | 1 | 0.75 |

Both profiles integrate to 840 multiplier-seconds, so their expected offered volumes match
for every topology, pressure and template. The two bursts differ in duration and peak height
from the historical single flash crowd. Actual event counts remain stochastic. The declared
arrival cutoff stops traffic; no schedule entry at the exact cutoff is needed.

HPA runs every 6 s at target busy/requested CPU ratio 0.6, tolerance 0.1, with 120 s scale-down
stabilization. The rebalancer allows two moves per 30 s window, subject to the total caps above.
Idle suspension threshold is 30 s, with the same published fog transition model; far-edge and
cloud nodes remain non-suspendable. Scheduler bind latency is 0.01 s, with zero injected
failure, informer delay and cycle overhead; score quantization and bind capacity checking stay
enabled. All other defaults are explicit in the generated files. Do not run their placeholder
base energy coefficients as a final condition: apply one of the three verified development
bundles and check the effective config identity in `DESIGN.json`.

## Calibration before choosing the primary operating conditions

The finite grid is 12 cells × 9 fixed references × 3 development bundles × 3 calibration seeds
= **972 episodes**. Use all seven original references and both previously disclosed posthoc
references; exact paths and source hashes are fixed in `DESIGN.json`. No evolved champion or
new proposal operator is used for this calibration. Report every reference/cell/bundle/seed,
including errors, infeasibility and indistinguishable values. Shared calibration seeds support
paired traffic comparisons; they do not create independent research replicates.

For each topology, first consider pressure 0.30. It qualifies as a primary operating level only
if at least one *single* fixed reference satisfies all constraints across both traffic profiles,
all three bundles and all three calibration seeds (18 episodes). If none does, consider 0.15
using the same rule. Do not substitute different references per episode to create a qualifying
oracle. Pressure 0.45 is a retained stress condition, never a fallback primary level.

This order prefers the declared middle workload and allows one prespecified lighter fallback.
Qualification uses service delivery, not energy rank, LLM/GP separation, or failure of the
strong simple rule. Record every qualifying reference. If neither primary level qualifies for
either topology, stop the proposed family study and document a design amendment with new
calibration/final grids; do not choose new pressures or deadlines silently. A technical worker
failure is not a measured infeasible policy: stop and diagnose the failure first.

The final B study would use both profiles at each topology's admitted level, with search and
evaluation seeds disjoint from calibration. Keep the full twelve-cell fixed-policy evaluation
as a descriptive operating-envelope/stress report. Do not pool that report with independent
search roots or label calibration data as final validation. The strong simple reference enters
both adaptive arms' twelve-program initialization (seven original references, the strong rule,
four seeded mutants). Search count, final grids, exact endpoints and executable scientific
freeze remain outstanding under `DESIGN.md`; this document does not launch that study.
